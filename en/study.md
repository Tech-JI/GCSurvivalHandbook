# Academic Life

## Honor Code

The **Honor Code**, commonly abbreviated as **HC**, defines the ethical standards expected of everyone at GC — all undergraduate and graduate students, faculty, staff, and administrators enrolled in the institute's courses.

The Honor Code is built on the following principles:

- Every engineer, whether a student or a professional, must act with personal integrity to ensure safety, health, fairness, and the proper use of all resources.
- All GC members are **honest and trustworthy**.
- Students, faculty, and staff at GC must **trust one another**, uphold the tenets of the **Honor Code**, and share collective responsibility for any violations of it.
- Academic achievement gained **without effort** is unacceptable.

**Reference**: [https://gc.sjtu.edu.cn/en/academics/academic-integrity/honor-code/](https://gc.sjtu.edu.cn/en/academics/academic-integrity/honor-code/)

## TA (Teaching Assistant)

**Student teaching assistants are a crucial part of GC's curriculum.**

For each course, the professor takes the lead on lectures, while **TAs** (Teaching Assistants) handle many non-lecture teaching tasks, including but not limited to: running **OH** (Office Hours) and **RC** (Recitation Class) — both explained below — grading assignments and exams, proctoring exams, and organizing exam paper review sessions. Students can also turn to TAs for help when they encounter difficulties in a course.

## OH (Office Hours)

**OH**, short for **Office Hour**, is a weekly drop-in Q&A session run by TAs and professors. If you run into any trouble with a course — whether it's keeping up with lectures, struggling with homework, or anything else — you can visit **Office Hour** and ask a **TA** or professor for help. You'll receive patient and thorough answers (as long as they stay within Honor Code boundaries).

## RC (Recitation Class)

**RC**, short for **Recitation Class**, is a review session led by a **TA**. It helps students consolidate the knowledge covered in recent lectures and provides practice problems to prepare for exams. For most courses, RC sessions are held once every 1–2 weeks. Before midterms and finals, there are also special **"Big RC"** sessions that walk through all the exam-relevant content and offer extra review exercises.

RC is entirely optional — TAs run these sessions out of their own initiative to give students extra review opportunities, so you can decide whether to attend based on your own needs. Most RC sessions also provide recordings, which is helpful for students who can't attend in person.

### **Math Course Selection: MATH1560 vs MATH1860**

Mathematics is one of the most important foundational subjects in your first year. GC offers two math pathways:

| Pathway | Course Series | Style |
|---------|--------------|-------|
| MATH1560 → 2550 → 2560 | Honors Calculus | **American-style**, starting from concrete problems with an emphasis on understanding and application |
| MATH1860 → 2850 → 2860 | Honors Mathematics | **European-style**, starting from mathematical theory frameworks with an emphasis on rigorous proofs |

**MATH1560 Series (Honors Calculus)**: More application-oriented, introducing new concepts through concrete examples and then expanding on them. Theorems are rigorously derived but complex proofs may be omitted. The focus is on applying mathematical results to solve practical problems. Suitable for students who prefer to "understand how to use it first, then understand why."

**MATH1860 Series (Honors Mathematics)**: A more rigorous mathematics course where most theorems are formally proved in class, developing new ideas from previous theory. Involves more linear algebra content. Suitable for students who prefer understanding mathematical concepts through proofs and those interested in pursuing mathematics or theoretical directions in depth.

> **Note**: Both pathways are equivalent in credits and graduation requirements. There is no "better" option — only "which suits you better." MATH1560 can transition to MATH2550 (if you took 1860 and there are open spots), but **5-series cannot transfer to 8-series**. MATH1160 (Regular Calculus II) is only offered in the spring semester.

## Course Selection Guide

**Fall Semester**: Complete the following foundational courses. For math, choose between **MATH1560/1860** (2.5 sessions/week). Take **CHEM2100** for chemistry (2.5 sessions/week). For introductory engineering, choose either **ENGR1010** or **ENGR1510**, or take **ENGR1000** (2 lectures + 1 lab session/week). Also fit in English writing **ENGL1000** (2.5 sessions/week), Modern Chinese History (1 session/week), Current Affairs & Policy (3 sessions/semester), and Physical Education (1 session/week).

**Spring Semester**: Continue with core courses. English writing **ENGL2000** (3 sessions/week), Chemistry Lab **CHEM2110** (1 session/week, 5 sessions total). Also take Psychology (1 session every 2 weeks), Military Theory (2 sessions/week), Current Affairs & Policy (3 sessions/semester), Moral Education (2 sessions/week, each session is a 3-period block), and PE (1 session/week, continuing into the first 3 weeks of summer).

**Summer Semester**: Focus on bridging into your major. Depending on your fall choices, complete the remaining portion of Intro to Computing / Intro to Engineering (2 lectures + 1 lab session/week). Take Physics Lab **PHYS1410** (1 session every 2 weeks, 5 sessions total). For math, choose between **MATH2550/2850** (2.5 sessions/week — note: if you took 1860 and there are spots, you can switch to 2550, but 5-series cannot transfer to 8-series). For physics, choose between **PHYS1500/1600** (Regular Physics / Enhanced Physics, 2.5 sessions/week). Humanities courses can be taken at any time (spring or summer recommended). Refer to the official graduation requirements to make sure you have enough credits in each category.

**Class of 2025 onward** will have a new mandatory general course: **National Security Education**.

### **ENGR1000 vs ENGR1010: Which to Take First in Fall?**

- **ENGR1000 (Introduction to Engineering)**: Divided into engineering technology and technical communication components. Students work in teams to complete a multi-stage engineering project, culminating in a showcase at **Design Expo**. The workload is relatively heavy but can be shared through teamwork. Suitable for students who enjoy hands-on practice and collaboration.
- **ENGR1010 (Introduction to Computing)**: Learn how computers work and programming (MATLAB, C, C++), with weekly labs and homework. Suitable for students with some programming background or those who want to build solid programming skills.

> About two-thirds of students take ENGR1010 first in the fall semester, but the two courses have no prerequisite relationship. The programming content in ENGR1000 is very basic — no prior programming experience is needed. Both are introductory courses and have no direct bearing on your future major choice (ME vs ECE). Just pick whichever interests you more.

### **Intellectual Breadth (IB) Course Categories**

**Intellectual Breadth (IB)** is an important component of GC graduation requirements, totaling **16 credits** (including 2 credits of Professional Ethics). IB courses fall into the following categories:

| Category | Minimum Requirement | Examples |
|----------|-------------------|----------|
| **Humanities** | At least **3 credits** | Philosophy, English Language & Literature, Asian Languages & Cultures, Comparative Literature, Film Studies, etc. |
| **Social Sciences** | At least **3 credits** | International & Comparative Studies, Political Science, Economics, Environmental Studies, History, etc. |
| **Professional Development** | ENGR4960J (2 credits) required | Engineering Ethics, Business, Entrepreneurship, etc. |
| **Natural Sciences** | No separate minimum | Biology, Chemistry, Physics, Astronomy, Earth Sciences, etc. |

Additionally, up to **8 credits** of language courses can count toward IB. Art courses have a separate minimum requirement of at least **2 credits**, which can count toward either IB or General Elective. Humanities courses are recommended in spring or summer semesters. Refer to the official graduation requirements before course selection to ensure you meet the credit thresholds in each category.

## Registration Guide

1. **Registration website**:
   [https://coursesel.gc.sjtu.edu.cn](https://coursesel.gc.sjtu.edu.cn)

2. **Registration timeline**
   Course registration generally takes place before the semester starts, during the break. The exact dates vary from year to year, but all notifications will be posted on **Canvas**. **Make absolutely sure you don't miss or mix up the registration deadline** — in certain cases (explained below), this can seriously hurt your course selection options.

3. **Available courses**
   The courses offered each semester will be announced on **Canvas** before registration begins. **Check Canvas regularly — seriously!** Also, the schedule (which courses are offered, their time slots, etc.) often changes between the first draft release and the start of registration, and updated versions will also be posted on **Canvas**.

4. **Registration methods**

   - **Lucky Draw**: Random lottery selection by the system.
   - **Early Bird**: First-come, first-served. Slots are released at random intervals (system protection mechanism).
   - **Assigned**: System-allotted enrollment, mainly used for parallel sections of mandatory courses to improve efficiency and prevent conflicts.
   - **Waitlist**: Queue-based backup.

5. **Dropping courses**

   - **Weeks 0–2**
     Free drop (except for courses with special restrictions).
   - **Weeks 3–4**
     Requires an application approved by the instructor and the program head.
   - **After week 4**
     Requires an application approved by the instructor, the program head, and the Undergraduate Committee.
   - **After week 6**
     Requires an application approved by the instructor, the program head, and the Undergraduate Committee. A "W" (Withdrawal) will appear on your transcript.

   **Note: You cannot drop a course once final assessments have begun or after grades have been posted. The rules above apply only to courses offered by this institute!**

6. **Common terminology**

   - **UEO**: Undergraduate Education Office. Contact them for any course-registration-related questions.
   - **bpm**: The platform for handling credit-related applications. Link: [https://bpm.gc.sjtu.edu.cn](https://bpm.gc.sjtu.edu.cn)
   - **Workload**: The range of institute-offered credits you must take each semester — must be between 12 and 18 credits.
   - **Overload**: Apply to the UEO before registration to take more than 18 credits in a single semester.
   - **Underload**: The opposite of overload — apply to take fewer than 12 credits. To apply for overload or underload, submit a request on bpm to the UEO before registration begins.
   - **Prerequisite**: A course you must have completed before enrolling in a more advanced one. For example, MATH255 requires MATH156 as a prerequisite — meaning you must have earned credit for MATH156 before you can take MATH255.
   - **Co-requisite**: A course you must be taking simultaneously (or have already passed) alongside another course. A typical example is ECE215 (sophomore year), which has VP250/260 as a co-requisite — you must enroll in VP250 in the same semester to take ECE215.
   - **Override Prerequisite**: A waiver that lets you bypass a prerequisite or co-requisite for a course you want to take. For example, if MATH255 requires MATH156 but you haven't taken MATH156, you can apply for an override to still enroll in MATH255. **Applications must also be submitted on bpm before registration begins.**

7. **Other notes**

   - If your credit load allows, you can add courses you didn't get into to a **waitlist** on the registration website. There's a chance you'll eventually get a spot (notified via your SJTU email, not Canvas).
   - When adding, dropping, or swapping courses, always check that your **workload** stays between 12 and 18 credits. You may not be able to complete these operations if you're outside that range.
   - The registration system strictly enforces time conflict rules. Even if two courses overlap for just one session across the entire semester, you cannot take both simultaneously.

## **Research Opportunities**

GC encourages undergraduate students to get involved in research as early as possible. Here are common on-campus research programs (ordered by timeline):

- **PRP** (Participation in Research Program): A university-wide undergraduate research program at SJTU where you work with a professor or graduate student on a research topic. Applications open each semester, suitable for second-semester freshmen and above.
- **IPP** (Innovation Practicum Program): GC's institute-level innovation practice program, emphasizing independent innovation and practical application.
- **Chun-Tsung Program (䇹政)**: An undergraduate research apprenticeship program established by the Chun-Tsung and Tsung-Dao Lee Foundation. It is competitive, with selected participants receiving research funding support.
- **IAP** (Independent Academic Project): A self-directed academic project where you can independently conduct research on a topic of interest.

Research outcomes from these programs can typically count toward the **general elective credits** required for graduation (up to 4 credits). Additionally, you can directly reach out to professors you're interested in working with and ask if there are opportunities to join their lab — this is the most direct and flexible path.

## Labor Hours

**Labor hours** are tied to labor education — an important part of university life and a graduation requirement. SJTU requires every student to complete at least **32** labor hours before graduating. That number may sound intimidating, but it's actually quite easy to fulfill because there are many ways to earn labor hours: **volunteer service**, **student organization activities**, **volunteer teaching** (organized by the Youth Volunteer Team), various small events and competitions (like UPC), and more. Just **make sure you don't forget to earn enough labor hours**! Generally, the institute is generous with awarding them, so meeting the requirement is not difficult. Plus, the activities you participate in give you far more than just labor hours — they offer valuable experiences and skills.

To check how many labor hours you've already earned, go to: **My SJTU (交我办) -- Study at SJTU -- Earned Labor Education Hours** (or check your **Second Classroom Transcript** for a comprehensive overview of all your related records).

### **GPA and Academic Standing**

#### **GPA Calculation**

GC uses a 4.0 GPA scale:

| Grade | GPA Points | Grade | GPA Points |
|-------|-----------|-------|-----------|
| A+    | 4.0       | C+    | 2.3       |
| A     | 4.0       | C     | 2.0       |
| A-    | 3.7       | C-    | 1.7       |
| B+    | 3.3       | D     | 1.0       |
| B     | 3.0       | P (Pass) | Not counted in GPA |
| B-    | 2.7       | F (Failure) | 0       |

**GPA Formula**: Semester GPA = Σ(Course GPA Points × Credits) / Σ(Credits). Cumulative GPA is calculated the same way across all courses taken.

#### **Grade Point Deficit (GPD) and Academic Status**

If your semester GPA or cumulative GPA falls below 2.0, you will incur a GPD (Grade Point Deficit).

- **Good Standing**: Both semester and cumulative GPA ≥ 2.0. Normal course registration.
- **Probation**: GPA between 1.0–2.0 (GPD 0–10). Must meet with your Program Advisor. Course load capped at 16 credits per semester.
- **Dismissal Warning**: Three consecutive probations, or GPA below 1.0 (GPD ≥ 10). Registration is prohibited. Must submit a reinstatement application to the Associate Dean for Undergraduate Education.

> **Note**: When retaking a course, all attempts appear on the transcript, but only the most recent grade counts toward GPA. A GPA of 2.0 is required for graduation. If you have questions about a grade, you can request a review from the instructor within one week after grades are posted (except for final exams).

#### **Course Withdrawal and W Notation**

| Time Period | Requirements |
|------------|-------------|
| **Weeks 0–2** | Free drop (except for specially designated courses) |
| **Weeks 3–4** | Requires application approved by the instructor and program head |
| **Week 5 onward** | Requires approval by the instructor, program head, and Undergraduate Committee |
| **After week 6** | A "W" (Withdrawal) is recorded on the transcript |
| **After final assessment begins** | No withdrawal applications accepted |

The above rules apply to all courses offered by GC. Special circumstances require supporting documentation.
